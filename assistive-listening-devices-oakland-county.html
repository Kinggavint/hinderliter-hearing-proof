Assistive device, hearing aid, or personal sound amplifier?

These terms describe different kinds of support. An assistive listening device is usually designed around a particular source or environment. A hearing aid is selected and fitted for an individual's hearing loss. The FDA says personal sound amplification products are consumer electronics for people with normal hearing in specific situations, not substitutes for hearing aids.

When a professional evaluation helps

If hearing difficulty is new, worsening, one-sided, associated with tinnitus, or affecting several settings, start with a hearing evaluation. ASHA describes what an adult hearing evaluation may include, including case history, testing, and discussion of next steps.

Before choosing an accessory

Write down the hardest listening situations. Bring hearing-aid make and model information, your phone model, or a photo of the TV connection panel when possible. Ask about compatibility, setup, maintenance, and what happens if the device does not meet the listening goal.

Assistive listening device FAQs

What are assistive listening devices?

Assistive listening devices, or ALDs, are tools that make selected sounds clearer in situations such as TV listening, a conversation in noise, a meeting, a phone call, or a home alert. They can work with or without hearing aids, depending on the device and the listening goal.

Can I use an assistive listening device without hearing aids?

Yes. Many assistive listening devices can be used with or without hearing aids. The right choice depends on the listening situation, the amount of background noise, and whether you need help with a TV, phone, conversation, or alerts.

What assistive listening device helps with a TV?

A TV listening system can send television audio to a receiver, headphones, or compatible hearing aids. Check the TV connection, the receiver style, and compatibility before choosing a device.

What helps me hear one speaker in a noisy room?

A remote microphone can pick up one speaker's voice near the source and send it to a receiver or compatible hearing aids. Ask a hearing professional whether the system fits your hearing technology and listening situation.

Are assistive listening devices the same as hearing aids?

No. An assistive listening device is designed to improve a specific listening situation. A hearing aid is a regulated medical device selected and fitted for a person's hearing loss. A professional hearing evaluation can help determine which type of support is appropriate.

Are personal sound amplification products the same as assistive listening devices?

No. FDA describes personal sound amplification products as consumer electronics for people with normal hearing in specific situations, not as substitutes for hearing aids. Assistive listening devices are intended to help make selected sounds clearer.
