Safe hearing-aid care checklist

The FDA and NIDCD both emphasize model-specific cleaning, protection from heat and moisture, regular inspection, and professional instruction. Work through this checklist only as far as it matches your device instructions.

  1. Power down before cleaning. Turn off the hearing aid or remove it from the charger using the manufacturer's normal process.
  2. Inspect the device. Look for visible wax, debris, moisture, a blocked microphone opening, a blocked sound outlet, or signs of damage.
  3. Use the supplied tools. Use the brush, cloth, wax-filter tool, or other supplies recommended for your model. Do not insert a sharp object or liquid into an opening.
  4. Keep it dry and cool. Avoid water, solvents, alcohol, household cleaners, direct sun, hot cars, ovens, microwaves, and hair dryers. Store the device as directed by the manufacturer.
  5. Check the power supply. Replace a dead disposable battery or seat a rechargeable device in its charger as directed. Keep batteries and small devices away from children and pets.
  6. Perform a listening check. After cleaning and powering the device as directed, listen for normal sound. If muffled sound, feedback, or another problem remains, contact a hearing professional.

The FDA hearing-aid care guidance advises users to clean as instructed, avoid water and solvents, avoid heat, inspect devices, and ask a hearing professional for care instruction. NIDCD hearing-aid guidance also notes that earwax and ear drainage can damage hearing aids.

When home care is not enough

Ask an audiologist for help when the device looks broken, cleaning does not restore sound, feedback continues, the fit is uncomfortable, a battery or charger behaves unusually, or you notice a change in hearing. A professional can check whether the problem involves wax, moisture, the receiver, fit, programming, or hardware.

Bring these details

  • Hearing-aid make and model
  • Disposable battery or rechargeable power
  • When the problem started
  • Whether one or both devices are affected
  • What you already tried

Request help promptly for

  • A visible crack, loose part, or damage
  • Persistent feedback or muffled sound
  • Pain or an uncomfortable fit
  • Moisture exposure or unusual heat
  • A sudden change in hearing

ASHA's hearing-aid troubleshooting handout recommends routine cleaning, keeping devices dry, checking power and sound openings, and calling an audiologist when a problem cannot be fixed at home.

Hearing-aid cleaning and care FAQs

How often should I clean my hearing aids?

Clean hearing aids according to the manufacturer's and audiologist's instructions. There is no single safe schedule for every model, because cleaning tools and removable parts differ. Inspect the devices regularly and ask your hearing professional to demonstrate the routine for your hearing aids.

Can I use water or alcohol to clean my hearing aids?

Do not use water, alcohol, solvents, or household cleaners on hearing aids unless the device manufacturer specifically instructs you to do so. The FDA warns that liquids and solvents can damage internal electronics. Use only the cleaning tools and method recommended for your model.

What should I do if my hearing aids sound muffled or whistle?

Check the power, charger or battery, microphone opening, sound outlet, and visible wax or debris using the tools and instructions supplied for your model. Keep the device dry. If muffled sound or feedback continues, schedule a hearing-aid check because wax, moisture, fit, receiver, or programming may be involved.

When should I bring hearing aids to an audiologist?

Ask for professional help when a device looks damaged, cleaning does not restore sound, feedback continues, the fit is uncomfortable, a battery or charger behaves unusually, or you notice a change in hearing. Bring the hearing-aid model and the steps you already tried.
