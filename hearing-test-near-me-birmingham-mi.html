What a diagnostic hearing evaluation includes

Short answer: A diagnostic hearing evaluation combines a conversation about your hearing, an ear exam, and hearing measurements that help your audiologist explain what may be affecting communication and what to do next.

  1. Case history: You can describe when the change started, which situations are difficult, and any relevant noise exposure, medical history, medications, tinnitus, dizziness, or ear symptoms.
  2. Ear inspection: The clinician looks at the outer ear and ear canal for findings that may affect testing or need follow-up.
  3. Hearing measurements: You respond to tones and other sounds so the clinician can measure hearing sensitivity and, when appropriate, speech understanding.
  4. Results and next steps: Your audiologist reviews what the results mean and explains whether monitoring, additional evaluation, or treatment may help.

Online and smartphone screening can be a starting point, but ASHA says it does not replace a diagnostic hearing evaluation by a licensed and certified audiologist.

What to look for when you need a hearing test

  • Doctor-led audiology (Au.D.) and clear recommendations you understand.
  • Diagnostic testing when appropriate, and a plan for next steps.
  • Real reviews from local patients and transparent scheduling.
  • Follow-up care that supports you after the test.

Common reasons people search “near me”

My hearing feels muffled. Should I schedule a test?

Muffled hearing can come from many causes, including earwax, fluid, or changes in hearing. A hearing evaluation can help clarify the cause and what to do next.

I hear ringing (tinnitus). Can an audiologist help?

Yes. Many tinnitus cases are related to hearing changes. An evaluation helps identify contributing factors and options for management.
