Hearing loops and assistive listening systems in Oakland County

Short answer: A hearing loop sends sound to a telecoil or separate receiver in a designated listening area. Public venues may also use FM or infrared systems. The best choice depends on the venue, the receiver provided, your hearing technology, and the listening setting.

Hinderliter Hearing Services is an audiology practice in Birmingham serving patients throughout Oakland County. This guide explains what to ask before a meeting, performance, worship service, class, or other public event. It does not confirm that a particular venue has a working system.

How the main systems differ

NIDCD describes hearing loops, FM, and infrared as types of assistive listening devices. All three can help bridge distance between a sound source and a listener, but the way they deliver sound affects what a visitor should check.

SystemHow it sends soundWhat a visitor should check
Hearing loopAn electromagnetic signal travels through a loop around the listening area.Whether your hearing aid or implant has a telecoil, or whether the venue provides a loop receiver and headset.
FM systemA transmitter sends sound by radio to a tuned receiver.The receiver channel, battery or charge, and the coupling option for your hearing technology.
Infrared systemAn emitter sends sound using infrared light to a receiver.Receiver availability, line of sight, and whether bright sunlight or an obstruction may affect use.
Personal remote microphoneA nearby speaker's voice is sent to compatible hearing aids or a receiver.Device compatibility, microphone placement, connection method, and who will wear the microphone.

ASHA identifies hearing loops, FM, and infrared as assistive listening technologies. The U.S. Access Board's technical overview also notes that system coverage and compatibility can vary.

Public-venue checklist before you arrive

Venue systems are not interchangeable, and availability can change. Call or check the venue's accessibility information before the event and ask:

System and receiver

  • Do you have a hearing loop, FM system, infrared system, or another option?
  • Do I need a telecoil, or does the venue provide a receiver and headset?
  • Where do I collect, charge, or test the receiver?
  • Is the system available at every seat or only in a designated area?

Backup access

  • Are captions, an assistive listening receiver, or accessible seating available?
  • Can staff test the system before the event begins?
  • Should I bring my hearing-aid make and model information?
  • Who should I contact if the sound is unclear during the event?

If your hearing aids have a telecoil, your hearing professional can explain how it is activated and what to expect in a looped setting. Not every hearing aid includes a telecoil, and a telecoil setting may need to be programmed or adjusted for your needs.

Hearing loop and assistive listening FAQs

What is a hearing loop?

A hearing loop is an assistive listening system that sends sound through a wire loop as an electromagnetic signal. A hearing aid or cochlear implant with a telecoil may receive the signal, or a venue can provide a separate receiver and headset.

Can I use a hearing loop with any hearing aid?

Not every hearing aid has a telecoil, and a telecoil must be working and programmed for the listening setting. Ask the venue what receiver it provides and ask your hearing professional how to use the telecoil or another connection.

What is the difference between a hearing loop, FM, and infrared system?

A hearing loop uses an electromagnetic field, FM uses radio transmission, and infrared uses light from an emitter to a receiver. Each system has different coverage, receiver, line-of-sight, and compatibility considerations, so confirm the venue setup before you arrive.

What should I ask a venue about assistive listening?

Ask whether the venue has a hearing loop, FM, or infrared system; whether you need a telecoil or a separate receiver; where to collect or test the receiver; and whether captions or accessible seating are available. Venue systems and availability can change.
