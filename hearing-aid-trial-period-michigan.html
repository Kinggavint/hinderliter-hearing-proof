Hearing-aid trial periods in Michigan: what to ask before you buy

Short answer: A hearing-aid trial is your opportunity to use the devices in real listening situations, return for adjustments, and decide whether the fitting supports your goals. Trial length, return steps, fees, and included services should be written down before the fitting.

Hinderliter Hearing Services provides a 45-day trial on every new fitting from its Birmingham office for patients across Oakland County. This page is general education, not legal or medical advice.

Ask about a hearing-aid fitting

What a hearing-aid trial is designed to test

A hearing aid can sound different in a quiet room than it does at home, in a restaurant, at work, or on a phone call. A trial gives you time to notice how the fitting performs across the places and conversations that matter to you. It is also a period for professional follow-up, not a one-time pass-or-fail test.

The FDA recommends asking about the total cost, whether a trial or adjustment period is available, refund terms, nonrefundable fees, warranty coverage, repairs, and care before purchasing. Read the FDA hearing-aid buying guide.

Seven questions to ask before the fitting

  1. What are the exact dates? Ask when the trial starts, when it ends, and how weekends or shipping time affect the deadline.
  2. What is included? Confirm whether the price includes the evaluation, fitting, programming, verification, orientation, follow-up visits, and routine maintenance.
  3. How will the fitting be verified? Ask whether the provider uses real-ear or probe-microphone measures when appropriate and how your hearing test guides programming.
  4. What happens if the sound is too loud or unclear? Confirm who to call, how quickly adjustments are scheduled, and what kinds of changes are included.
  5. What are the return or exchange steps? Ask how to notify the office, what must be returned, whether fees are nonrefundable, and when a refund is issued.
  6. What does the warranty cover? Ask about repairs, loss or damage, moisture, loaner devices, charger coverage, and the warranty end date.
  7. How will success be measured? Name your real goals: hearing a spouse, following a meeting, using the phone, hearing in a restaurant, or reducing listening fatigue.

ASHA's adult hearing-aid guidance describes fitting as a process that includes assessment, selection, verification, orientation, and validation.

Short notes are more useful than trying to remember every listening moment. Bring a family member or communication partner when possible so they can share what changes they notice.

Hearing aids may improve access to speech, but they do not restore normal hearing or guarantee that every word will be understood in every environment. Outcomes depend on the hearing loss, fitting, room, distance, competing sound, and consistent use.

OTC versus prescription hearing-aid trial terms

ASHA notes that OTC hearing aids are intended for adults with perceived mild to moderate hearing loss and that OTC trial periods are determined by the manufacturer. Prescription hearing-aid return policies may be shaped by state rules, provider terms, manufacturer terms, or payer requirements. That is why the written terms matter more than a generic promise of a “trial.”

OTC devices

  • For adults 18 and older with perceived mild to moderate hearing loss.
  • Self-fitting and support vary by product.
  • Trial and return rules come from the manufacturer or seller.
  • One-sided hearing loss, sudden change, pain, drainage, vertigo, or one-sided tinnitus needs professional guidance.

Prescription fittings

  • Selected and programmed from a diagnostic hearing evaluation.
  • Professional fitting, verification, orientation, and follow-up can be part of the plan.
  • Ask for the trial, exchange, refund, warranty, and care terms in writing.
  • Hinderliter provides a 45-day trial on every new fitting.

See ASHA's OTC and prescription context and NIDCD's OTC hearing-aid guidance.

When to call for help during the trial

Call the audiologist when a device is uncomfortable, whistles, feels too loud, sounds unclear, will not stay in place, will not charge, or does not connect to a phone. Adjustments and coaching are part of learning whether the fitting is right.

Do not use a trial as a substitute for urgent medical care. Sudden or rapidly worsening hearing loss, ear pain or drainage, one-sided tinnitus, vertigo, or severe dizziness needs prompt medical guidance. The FDA lists these as warning signs that should be evaluated by a doctor or hearing professional.

Hearing-aid trial period FAQs

How long is a hearing-aid trial period?

There is no single trial length for every hearing aid. The provider, manufacturer, payer, and device type can affect the terms, so ask for the start date, end date, return process, and any nonrefundable fees in writing. Hinderliter Hearing Services provides a 45-day trial on every new fitting.

Does Michigan require a hearing-aid trial period?

Trial and return terms can depend on the hearing aid, provider, manufacturer, and payer. Do not rely on a verbal promise: ask for the written terms before the fitting, including the exact return deadline and any fees. Hinderliter Hearing Services provides a 45-day trial on every new fitting.

What should I do during a hearing-aid trial?

Use the hearing aids in the situations that matter to you, such as quiet conversations, restaurants, family gatherings, television, and phone calls. Keep short notes about clarity, comfort, feedback, battery or charging, and which settings need adjustment. Bring those notes to follow-up visits.

Can I return hearing aids during the trial period?

Return eligibility and refund details depend on the written trial terms. Before you take the devices home, ask how to start a return, whether fitting or restocking fees apply, what accessories must be returned, and how the deadline is calculated. Contact the practice promptly if the devices are uncomfortable or not meeting your goals.

What if my hearing aids are uncomfortable during the trial?

Call the audiologist rather than abandoning the trial. Comfort, feedback, loudness, retention, and sound quality can often be addressed with programming changes, earmold or dome changes, orientation, or a different style. Persistent pain, drainage, sudden hearing change, or severe dizziness needs medical guidance.

What should be included in a hearing-aid trial plan?

Ask about the hearing evaluation, device selection, fitting and verification, orientation, follow-up adjustments, trial dates, return or exchange terms, warranty, repairs, maintenance, accessories, and what happens after the trial. A complete plan should make the professional services as clear as the device price.
