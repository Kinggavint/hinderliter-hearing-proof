Checklist: what to look for in a top rated audiologist

  1. Verified credentials (education, licensure, and professional affiliations).

  3. Realistic, pressure-free recommendations based on your needs, not a one-size-fits-all pitch.
  4. Strong follow-up and adjustment plan for hearing aids and ongoing hearing health.
  5. Convenient access and communication (scheduling, parking, response time, and support).
  6. Consistent patient feedback in recent reviews, not only older reviews.

What a complete hearing evaluation should include

Hearing care should be based on testing, not guessing. A complete evaluation often includes:

  • A case history (your symptoms, noise exposure, medications, and health background)
  • Ear examination
  • Testing that measures how you hear different pitches and how well you understand speech
  • A clear explanation of results and what they mean for daily life
  • Next steps that match your goals

How to verify credentials in Michigan

If you want to verify an audiologist's credentials, you can ask directly about licensure and training, and confirm details through official sources. It is also reasonable to ask whether the audiologist is a Doctor of Audiology (Au.D.) and what continuing education they complete.

How can I verify an audiologist's license in Michigan?

Use Michigan's free online licensee search to check whether an audiologist is licensed, review the issue date and expiration date, and see whether disciplinary action is listed. Michigan says its licensing records are updated daily, but you should still confirm that the information is current and complete. For formal certified verification, use MiPLUS. FAQs

Do I need a referral to see an audiologist in Michigan?

Many patients can schedule an audiology evaluation directly, but insurance requirements vary. If you are unsure, ask the clinic what your plan typically requires.

What is the difference between an audiologist and a hearing aid store?

An audiologist is a licensed hearing healthcare professional trained to evaluate hearing and recommend treatment options. Some hearing aid retailers focus primarily on devices. The right choice depends on your goals, medical history, and the level of testing and follow-up you want.

How often should hearing aids be adjusted?

Adjustments are common early on. Many people benefit from a few follow-up visits in the first weeks to months so the settings match real-world listening needs.

Frequently asked questions

Michigan says its licensing records are updated daily, but you should still confirm that the information is current and complete. For formal certified verification, use MiPLUS.

Do I need a referral to see an audiologist in Michigan?

In most cases, you do not need a referral to schedule a hearing evaluation with an audiologist. If you plan to use insurance, we can help you confirm any referral requirements before your visit.

What is included in a complete hearing evaluation?

A complete evaluation typically includes a case history, an ear exam, hearing tests that measure how you hear tones and speech, and a clear explanation of results and next steps.

How long does a hearing test appointment take?

Plan for about 60 minutes for a first visit, depending on your concerns and whether you want time to review hearing aid options.

How do I compare hearing aid recommendations between clinics?

Ask what listening needs the recommendation is based on, what technology levels are available, what the trial period and follow-up schedule look like, and what services are included after the fitting.

Is it better to see a hearing aid dispenser or an audiologist?

Both can help with hearing aids, but an audiologist is trained to evaluate hearing and balance concerns, interpret test results, and coordinate care when medical follow-up is needed.

What should I bring to my appointment?

Bring a list of medications, any prior hearing tests, your insurance card if you are using insurance, and your current hearing aids if you already wear them.

Can tinnitus be evaluated during a hearing test?

Yes. Tinnitus is often related to hearing loss, and a hearing evaluation is a good first step. We can discuss management options based on your symptoms and test results.
