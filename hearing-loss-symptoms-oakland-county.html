Hearing loss symptoms and screening in Oakland County

Short answer: Repeatedly asking people to repeat themselves, raising the TV volume, missing words in background noise, or depending on captions can be a reason to schedule a hearing screening or diagnostic hearing evaluation. These signs do not tell you the cause or severity by themselves.

Common signs worth checking

Hearing changes can be gradual, so people sometimes notice the effect on daily life before they notice a clear change in loudness. Consider a hearing screening or evaluation if you often:

Conversation signs

  • Ask others to repeat or speak more slowly.
  • Have trouble following group conversations or restaurant talk.
  • Feel that people are mumbling even when they seem loud enough.
  • Watch faces closely or rely on captions to fill in missed words.

Everyday listening signs

  • Turn up the TV or phone more than other people prefer.
  • Miss doorbells, timers, alarms, or someone calling from another room.
  • Avoid phone calls, meetings, or social settings because listening feels tiring.
  • Notice ringing, fullness, or a change in one or both ears.

NIDCD's hearing-test guide offers questions to consider before seeking help. A symptom list can start a conversation, but only an appropriate clinical assessment can explain what is happening.

Hearing screening or diagnostic evaluation?

ASHA describes hearing screening as a check for risk that can identify people who may benefit from further assessment. A screening is not the same as a full hearing evaluation.

A five-step next-action path

  1. Write down the situations that are hardest. Note TV, phone, one-to-one conversation, groups, or listening in noise.
  2. Ask someone you trust what they notice. A family member may notice missed words or volume changes that are easy to overlook.
  3. Choose a screening or evaluation. Share whether the concern is gradual, one-sided, sudden, or connected to tinnitus or an ear symptom.
  4. Bring useful details. List medications, noise exposure, prior tests, hearing devices, and questions about communication goals.
  5. Leave with a clear plan. That plan may be monitoring, communication strategies, a medical referral, or hearing technology discussion based on the findings.

Hearing loss symptom FAQs

What are common signs of hearing loss in adults?

Common signs include asking people to repeat themselves, turning up the TV, missing parts of conversations in noise, feeling that people mumble, or relying more on captions. These signs do not diagnose hearing loss, but they are good reasons to schedule a hearing screening or evaluation.

What is the difference between a hearing screening and a hearing evaluation?

A hearing screening is a brief check to identify whether someone may need more assessment. A diagnostic hearing evaluation is more detailed and combines history, ear examination, listening tests, and interpretation. A screening does not replace a diagnostic evaluation when hearing difficulty is suspected.

When should I schedule a hearing test?

Schedule a hearing test when hearing difficulty affects conversations, work, safety, phone calls, or several listening settings, or when family members notice a change. An audiologist can explain whether a screening, diagnostic evaluation, monitoring, or another next step fits your situation.

What should I do about a sudden change in hearing?

A sudden or rapidly changing hearing problem needs prompt medical attention. Contact a medical professional or urgent care service for guidance rather than waiting for a routine appointment. This educational page is not a diagnosis or emergency service.
