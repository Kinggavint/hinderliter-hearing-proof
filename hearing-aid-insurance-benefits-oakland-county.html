Medicare hearing-aid coverage: separate the exam from the device

Medicare.gov states that Original Medicare does not cover hearing aids or exams for fitting hearing aids. Beneficiaries pay the costs of those non-covered services. Medicare Advantage plans may offer extra hearing benefits, but the specific allowance, network, device rules, and cost-sharing come from the plan.

Medicare.gov explains that Part B may cover diagnostic hearing and balance exams when a doctor or other health care provider orders them to determine whether medical treatment is needed. That is different from a routine exam for selecting or fitting hearing aids. Before booking, ask what type of appointment is being ordered and what your plan requires.

Ten questions to ask the insurer

  1. Does my plan cover a diagnostic hearing evaluation, and is a referral or prior authorization required?
  2. Does my plan cover hearing aids, or only the evaluation?
  3. Is the benefit an allowance, a percentage, a fixed copay, or a discount program?
  4. Is there a separate hearing-benefit administrator or approved vendor?
  5. Must I use an in-network audiologist, manufacturer, or dispensing provider?
  6. How often can I use the benefit, and when does the benefit year reset?
  7. Are fitting, real-ear verification, orientation, programming, and follow-up included?
  8. Are repairs, loss or damage, chargers, batteries, accessories, and earmolds covered?
  9. Do I need a written order, test results, or a medical-necessity statement?
  10. What will I owe after the plan pays, and can you give me a reference number?

Write down the representative's name, the date, and the reference number. Ask for a summary of benefits or written authorization when available. A benefit quote can change if the provider, device, service code, network, or authorization changes.

Hearing-aid insurance FAQs

Does Original Medicare pay for hearing aids?

Original Medicare does not cover hearing aids or exams for fitting hearing aids. Medicare says beneficiaries pay the costs of these non-covered services. Some Medicare Advantage plans may offer extra hearing benefits, so check the specific plan for its allowance, network rules, authorization steps, and cost-sharing.

Does Medicare cover a diagnostic hearing test?

Medicare Part B may cover diagnostic hearing and balance exams when a doctor or other health care provider orders them to determine whether medical treatment is needed. Coverage rules differ from a routine exam for fitting hearing aids. Confirm the reason for the visit, order requirements, participating provider rules, and your expected cost before the appointment.

Do Medicare Advantage plans cover hearing aids?

Some Medicare Advantage plans offer extra hearing benefits that Original Medicare does not. The benefit can vary by plan, year, network, device allowance, vendor, and authorization rules. Call the number on the plan card and ask what is covered, where you must obtain the device, and what you pay.

Will private insurance pay for hearing aids?

Private insurance and employer plans vary. A plan may cover a diagnostic evaluation, a hearing-aid allowance, related services, or none of these. Ask the insurer about the benefit year, deductible, copay, network, authorization, device limits, fitting services, trial or return terms, and whether the benefit is administered by a separate hearing program.

What should I ask my insurance company about hearing aids?

Ask whether your plan covers diagnostic hearing evaluations, hearing aids, fitting and verification, follow-up visits, repairs, accessories, and replacement devices. Also ask about the dollar allowance, frequency limit, in-network requirements, prior authorization, claim submission, and non-covered fees. Write down the representative's name, date, and reference number.
